**Project:** Front Porch and Entry Stairs Maintenance Repaint  
**Property:** 10375 SW Dunlin Pl, Beaverton, OR 97007  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-25  
**Purpose:** Internal estimating record; not customer facing.

## Recommendation

- **Fixed customer price:** **$1,450**
- **Labor:** 20 crew hours
- **Materials and sundries allowance:** $225
- **Crew plan:** two painters over approximately two working days, with elapsed time governed by washing, drying, recoating, and walking-surface cure

The corrected photo takeoff is materially smaller than the earlier working model. The selling price remains $1,450 because this small exterior project carries meaningful fixed costs: Beaverton transportation, mobilization, protection, washing, separate coating stages, four distinct coating purchases, detailed rail work, white/gray transitions, cleanup, return visits, minor repair uncertainty, overhead, and margin.

## Pricing and Margin

```text
Labor cost             20 hours x $25 = $500
Materials and sundries                = $225
Estimated direct cost                 = $725
Fixed customer price                  = $1,450
Contribution above direct cost        = $725
Contribution margin                   = 50.0%
Effective revenue per labor hour      = $72.50
Price less materials per labor hour   = $61.25
```

The higher margin created by the corrected geometry is reported directly. It is appropriate for a small, multi-stage exterior project with Beaverton travel, several visits, separate product minimums, weather/drying risk, detail-heavy railing work, two-color transitions, minor repair uncertainty, and normal business overhead. The dimensions, labor, and materials are not increased to reverse-engineer the approved price.

## Prior-Project Check

The $1,450 price remains $1,050 below the more difficult $2,500 front-and-back porch project. That project included substantially more surface area, three colors, ceiling and enclosure work, broader priming, and two finish coats. Dunlin is smaller and simpler, but still requires the same basic mobilization, multiple coating stages, and exterior scheduling discipline.

## Production Sequence

1. Confirm colors, scope boundaries, coating compatibility, and alternate entry.
2. Protect adjacent surfaces and landscaping.
3. Wash included surfaces and allow full dry-down.
4. Complete localized scraping, sanding, filling, and minor paintable repairs.
5. Apply one complete tinted primer coat to gray surfaces and spot-prime bare/repaired white areas.
6. Apply one complete white finish coat and one complete gray finish coat, respecting recoat and cure times.
7. Complete two-color detailing and touch-ups; demask, clean, and walk through.

## Risks and Field Checks

- Field-measure the 10.0 x 4.25 ft porch and confirm the landing is fully rectangular.
- Measure all porch and stair rail sections; count balusters and posts directly.
- Confirm no stringer, rim, lattice, ceiling, house, door, concrete, underside, or framing work is expected.
- Probe damaged rail ends and caps. Carpentry, rot repair, structural work, and replacement remain excluded.
- Confirm existing-coating compatibility, substrate moisture, weather window, and alternate entry.
