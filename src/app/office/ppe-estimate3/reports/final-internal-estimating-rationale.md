## Preparation and Production Notes

1. Confirm room dimensions, exact scope, colors, and both sides of the door before setup.
2. Confirm that the vanity, sink, and toilet remain removed and that plumbing is secure.
3. Protect the resilient plank floor and doorway; mask adjacent hall surfaces as needed.
4. Remove switch/outlet plates if safe and authorized; otherwise mask. Protect plumbing, window glass/film, vent, light, hinges, knob, and latch.
5. Clean with a residue-free method suitable for painted bathroom surfaces. Rinse/wipe as required and allow to dry.
6. Scuff sand glossy surfaces with fine abrasive or sanding sponge. Use liquid deglosser only where appropriate and compatible with the coating system.
7. Fill ordinary holes with suitable patching compound. Feather sand without flattening surrounding texture; remove all dust.
8. Spot prime repairs. Apply a compatible high-adhesion bonding primer over the glossy dark blue surfaces, with continuous primer on the upper walls/ceiling for adhesion and color control.
9. Apply selective paintable caulk at minor open trim joints after cleaning and before finish coats. Do not caulk weep paths, movable joints, or inappropriate window components.
10. Apply two satin finish coats to ceiling and upper drywall in warm cream.
11. Apply two semi-gloss finish coats to beadboard, cap rail, baseboards, window trim, door casing, and the entire six-panel door in dark terracotta.
12. Maintain wet edges and inspect beadboard grooves, panel profiles, corners, and prior patch locations for misses.
13. Demask, touch up, clean, complete walkthrough, label retained paint, and document final colors/products.

## Coverage and Color-Change Assumptions

- One bonding-primer coat plus two finish coats is the base system.
- Primer is required for adhesion over the existing glossy finish and to reduce show-through from dark blue to warm cream.
- Two finish coats are carried for both colors.
- Warm cream should cover normally over a uniform appropriate primer.
- Dark terracotta color depth and base selection must be checked when ordering. A highly translucent match may require an additional coat.
- Sheen can emphasize patch and texture differences; repairs are minor and feathered, not full-wall skim coated.
- Final color approval must be based on physical samples under room lighting.

The effective markup is higher than the 29.4% example in `ppe-estimate2` because this is a very small one-day project with three coating purchases, mobilization, procurement, administrative time, and return-visit exposure. The $600 price remains fair: direct labor is efficient due to fixture removal, while the quote still covers professional prep and separate bathroom-grade systems rather than treating the room as a simple one-color wall repaint.

## Risk Notes and Potential Change Orders

- Major drywall repair, torn paper, large anchor damage, full skim coating, or texture matching beyond localized feather repairs.
- Water damage, mold/mildew remediation, plumbing leakage, or failed substrate discovered after cleaning.
- Additional coats required by a highly translucent final color or a material color change after purchase/application.
- Customer requests to paint the light fixture, vent grille, electrical devices, plumbing components, window frame/glass/film, or adjacent hall surfaces.
- Door scope differing from both faces and paintable edges, or hardware removal/reinstallation requiring repair.
- Vanity, sink, or toilet present during production, adding masking/cutting or requiring trade coordination.
- Failed beadboard, loose trim, carpentry, window repair, or excessive caulking not visible in photos.
- Product incompatibility discovered through adhesion testing; oil/alkyd contamination or an unknown specialty coating may require a different primer.
- Drying conditions that require a separate return visit. Normal schedule flexibility is carried; extensive added mobilization is not.
