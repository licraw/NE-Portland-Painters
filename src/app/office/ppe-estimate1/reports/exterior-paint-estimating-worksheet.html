Phase 2 — Building Geometry Analysis

Building Type

Single-structure 1.5-story craftsman bungalow. There is no addition or separate structure. The building is a mostly rectangular house with a small bump-out at the rear-left (NW) corner.

Roof Form and Orientation

  • Gabled roof with ridge running parallel to the depth (east-west / front-to-rear direction).
  • Gable ends face LEFT (north) and RIGHT (south) — these are the tall, triangular walls running from grade to the ridge peak.
  • Front and rear elevations are the EAVE FACES — wall height is the eave height (~9 ft). No gable triangle on front or rear.
  • Two shed or gable dormers on the FRONT SLOPE of the roof, facing the street. These add upper-floor window area above the 9 ft eave line on the front slope.

Siding

  • Front and rear elevations: horizontal lap siding (aluminum, customer-confirmed).
  • Left (north) and right (south) gable end walls: vertical board-and-batten siding. The tall appearance of these walls in photos is because they run from grade all the way to the ridge peak (~17–19 ft total height).
  • Both siding types are on the same single building. The B&B is not a separate structure — it is the cladding on the gable end walls.

Front Porch

  • Covered front entry porch. Two round columns supporting the porch roof.
  • The porch is a 6 ft roof overhang projecting from the front wall — NOT an enclosed structure. There is no additional siding area behind the porch roof overhang beyond the main front wall.
  • Front wall (behind the porch): horizontal lap siding at 9 ft eave height. This is the normal front face of the house.
  • Porch ceiling (underside of the 6 ft roof): painted board material. SEVERE paint failure (see Phase 8).
  • Porch width: estimated ~18 ft (spans approximately 2/3 of the 27 ft front face). Field-verify.

Rear / Bump-Out

  • The rear-left (NW) corner of the house has a small bump-out that adds approximately 6 ft to the building width and 3 ft to the building depth.
  • Bump-out eave height: same as main body (~9 ft). No change in wall height.
  • Rear entry: steps down from the bump-out area. Small side deck also noted (outside of main footprint).
  • Rear trellis/pergola adjacent to bump-out. May be in painting scope — confirm with client.

Chimney

  • Brick masonry chimney at or near the right-front corner of the bungalow (right/south gable end).
  • Full masonry — NOT painted. Deducted from paintable area.
  • Estimated footprint: ~3 ft × 3 ft rising full height.

Phase 8 — Condition Assessment

IMPORTANT: Condition findings below affect prep labor estimates, not geometry calculations. These two scopes must be tracked separately.

front-porch-ceiling.JPEG

SEVERE / HIGH PREP. Paint failure is extensive — multiple layers curling and actively flaking from the horizontal board ceiling planks. Virtually the entire ceiling surface shows peeling. Moisture-driven failure likely. Expect:

  • Full surface scraping required across ~108 SF ceiling (field-verify actual dimensions).
  • Possible sanding to bare wood in worst areas.
  • Stain-blocking primer application before topcoat.
  • This surface alone may represent 3–5× normal prep multiplier vs. typical exterior surface.
  • Investigate moisture source (gutter overflow, condensation, roof leak) before closing out paint scope.

front-porch-fascia.JPEG

MODERATE PREP. Porch fascia shows surface weathering and minor paint film checking. No active peeling in this view. Standard prep (wash, light sand, prime chalky sections) adequate.

soffit.jpeg

MODERATE PREP. Soffit underside shows staining and paint film stress. No severe peeling. Possible past moisture intrusion. Standard prep adequate; spot-prime stained areas before topcoat.

front-door.JPEG

LOW–MODERATE PREP. Front door painted dark (black/navy). Paint appears intact. Minor scuffs. Standard prep (clean, degloss/sand) before repainting.

General Siding Condition — Board-and-Batten (Gable End Walls)

The vertical board-and-batten siding on the left and right gable end walls (visible in left-side and right-side photos) shows:

  • Significant chalking/oxidation — flat, chalky film indicating aged UV-degraded paint.
  • Some cracking at vertical batten edges and horizontal joints.
  • Light pressure-wash and spot-prime required before topcoat.
  • Some rust staining from fasteners in close-up shots.
  • Note: aluminum siding (customer-confirmed) must NOT be heavily sanded — this would damage the surface oxide layer and require an adhesion/bonding primer.

General Siding Condition — Horizontal Lap (Front and Rear)

Horizontal lap siding on the front and rear elevations shows:

  • Similar chalking/oxidation to gable wall siding.
  • No severe peeling visible on main body siding.
  • Standard prep (wash, hand-scrape any loose areas, prime bare spots) anticipated.

UNQUANTIFIED — FIELD SURVEY REQUIRED. Customer indicated window sill/base deterioration may require scraping, sanding, and spot bondo repair. Neither the photos nor satellite confirmed the count or severity. This is a labor wildcard — quantify at site visit.
