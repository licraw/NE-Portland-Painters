<p>In the Puget Sound, we experience rain for the majority of the year. Of course, this plays a significant role in planning exterior painting projects. &nbsp;Along with the moisture level outside, temperature should also be considered before painting.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">How Moisture Affects Paint</h4>



<p>Paint cures by evaporation of water/solvents, and too much moisture or extreme temperatures (too cold&nbsp;<span style="text-decoration: underline;"><i>or</i></span>&nbsp;too hot) inhibit this process. &nbsp;In the worst scenarios, this will cause the new coat of paint to blister or bubble. &nbsp;Blistering can occur even after a coat of paint has dried, if it is exposed to too much moisture, too soon.</p>



<p>This isn’t to say that exterior painting can only be done on a perfect day. If so, all of us painting contractors here in Seattle might be in trouble!</p>



<p class="has-text-align-center"><iframe loading="lazy" src="//www.youtube.com/embed/ChCSSeSlvXo" width="350" height="197" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen="allowfullscreen"></iframe><br><em>A typical day in Seattle.</em></p>



<p>In general, being cautious about when you decide to paint is the key to avoiding disaster. &nbsp;As always, make sure that all areas are probably prepared before painting. &nbsp;Since the first step for preparing an exterior is pressure washing, the painting surface should be completely dry before applying product. &nbsp;This generally takes about 3 days, and in less than ideal weather conditions, this may take longer. &nbsp;Also make sure to allow extra drying time (up to 2-3 times the regular amount) in between coats, as paint will take longer to dry as the days get colder and shorter. &nbsp;This also means that you will have less working hours during the day. You should stop working in the mid-afternoon, or around the time when the temperature starts to drop.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Temperature</h4>



<p>Finally, always check the <span style="text-decoration: underline;"><em>application temperature range</em></span> of your chosen product, and stay within that range. &nbsp;Fortunately, today’s exterior paints boast pretty impressive ranges, many of which allow you to paint in temperatures as low as 35-40 degrees F. &nbsp;Keep in mind that the temperature of the surface that you are painting may actually be lower than the temperature outside. &nbsp;Ideally, avoid painting in temperatures lower than 50 degrees F.</p>



<p>Typically, we paint exteriors from March to late October during sunnier/drier weather. &nbsp;It is always advisable to schedule well ahead of time, since painting contractors are typically very busy in the summer, and often even completely booked.</p>
